What it does

It watches public Twitch streams to find moments worth clipping, cuts short videos from them to local disk, and — once connected — reads the operator's own TikTok and YouTube account statistics and can send videos to the operator's own TikTok, YouTube and Instagram accounts.

A TikTok upload arrives as a draft the operator posts themselves; YouTube and Instagram uploads are published directly.

Platform rules

Using this tool does not exempt anyone from the rules of the platforms it connects to. The operator must comply with TikTok's, YouTube's, Meta's and Twitch's terms, developer policies and community guidelines.

Posting happens only through the platforms' official APIs, with credentials the account owner has granted. Nothing is ever posted by driving a website or an app.

Reading Twitch uses Twitch's API where credentials allow it, and otherwise reads pages and video that Twitch serves publicly: the channel page, to see whether someone is live, and yt-dlp, to fetch a VOD or a live stream to cut from. Those are not official APIs, and saying otherwise would be untrue.

In no case does the tool disguise automated access as human activity, rotate accounts or addresses, use more than one account per platform, or attempt to work around any limit, block or review. It identifies itself by name when it asks Twitch for a page. That is a deliberate design boundary, not an oversight, and it is documented in docs/detection-plan.md.
